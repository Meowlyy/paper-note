Chapter Three — What Panic Looks Like

What panic looks like

at too much stress — even just a little, like when someone near me gets loud or mad, or there's an argument — I get anxious, mega high pulse, and I shake a lot, and depending on the situation I can pass out. and I get panic attacks often, which isn't something to joke about :) like when I'm really really tired, in bed, trying to sleep, I often get panic attacks or I'm right on the edge of one.

social anxiety and so, etc etc, all my various traumas, etc. and you don't see most of it from outside until you push the person to a certain point, and then it's already too late... and no apology helps anymore at that point. and with the social anxiety, I stop perceiving anything around me, my pulse goes hollow, I get dizzy and everything goes blurry, and in situations like that, if I can, I position myself behind people who give me a bit of safety — that's been my brother in some situations, and not my mom, honestly, 🙃 but it always depends on the situation and the person, and minimal safe space fits better than 100%, because I'm not that open there — or really, none of us are.

and you really can't see from outside how bad a situation is for me in that moment — staring at the floor or whatever, not perceiving anything anymore, just wanting to get away, super high pulse and stress and dizziness, and sometimes forgetting to breathe for minutes. and no matter what, in all situations like that, you don't want to be talked at, even if it's meant nicely — that just makes it worse, and honestly in that moment the person also just wants to get away from everything. what you actually need is the comfy spot: a person who's there, gives you that comfy, feel-good space, stays close to you and looks out for you, sitting right next to you, close... like, really close.. and just a hug — but more a hug pulling you in and holding on, so you can snuggle into it.

and you can calm down, even if it's only a little, and if it's possible, take the person out of the situation without asking and without pushing — that can just be an "okay, come on," even if it's only a few people around, just walking a bit further to where no one's around and you both have quiet space again. and at the smallest thing — something you do or say, even if it's subconscious — it can make the person not want to come to you anymore, because the safe space got broken, and they don't feel comfortable, and forcing it just makes everything worse. at least one good thing though: when the person feels comfortable and trusts you, they can get really clingy and cuddly for that safe space, in pretty much any situation.

and you just have to see naturally, over time, how and what and where — it can be the smallest things. like with me, I don't like it when someone walks behind me (doesn't mean never walk behind me, more like — take me along at your side, walk forward together, or if it's a bit behind, be behind together). same with sitting — I always sit on the right, so left has to be my safe space, the other way around doesn't feel right. same with sleeping, or biking, being in front matters — same logic in every similar situation, sleeping, cuddling, lying down, whatever. and one thing that plays into it — right being the main hand and left the weaker one — kind of explains itself, i think :) and in calmness lies the strength.

but that doesn't mean I expect someone to do everything — no, I just need help, a little nudge, softer at night, but intensive help, soft, not direct, more interactive than instructive. and sometimes in stress situations my head just goes blank, and I forget stuff, like turning off the bathroom light, or other things, because my head just doesn't cooperate — and that can be really uncomfortable for me depending on the situation. what helps is just the comfy safe space again, not punishing the person for it with annoyed thoughts or anger or bad looks — that's the fastest way to lose that safe space for a long time, or for good. and be rewarded for the smallest things, even if they seem tiny or insignificant from outside — for the person, that might've taken a lot of energy and strength they probably didn't even have.

Chapter Four — Scars, Pain & Not Giving Up

Still standing

I also want to talk about moving forward. dwelling too much on the past isn't good — you need a clear view ahead, no matter how much it hurts or how many scars you carry.

despite everything I've been through, all the bad experiences and the traumas I still carry, I won't give up. I'll keep trying my best, no matter how much it hurts, and even if I cry at some point because of the pain inside me, I'll try to hold onto my smile and stay the good-hearted person I truly am.

just remember this: I won't forget you. there will always be a part of me where all of you have a safe place to stay.
